## GPA Calculator Compass

**Website:** [gpacalculator.website](https://gpacalculator.website/)

**Category:** Education · GPA guides · Academic planning  
**Language:** English

### What it offers

GPA Calculator Compass explains GPA calculations, credit weighting, and grading-scale differences. Its homepage currently presents an educational guide rather than an interactive calculator form. Listed reading topics include cumulative, weighted, unweighted, high-school, percentage-based, and final-exam GPA questions.

The guide distinguishes the arithmetic from institutional rules. It discusses how repeated courses, transfer credits, and different credit systems can complicate an estimate. That distinction is the main reason for including it here.

### Why we recommend exploring it

- **It encourages understanding before calculation.** A number is easier to evaluate when you can explain the assumptions behind it. We prefer that learning-first approach to treating an unexplained result as authoritative.
- **It gives readers a useful starting question.** Before comparing results, ask whether the same courses, credits, and grading scale were used. This is a productive habit to develop alongside any calculator.
- **It supports more purposeful reading.** Choose a topic that matches your question instead of reading every guide indiscriminately. We recommend starting with terminology, then moving to the calculation relevant to your situation.

### A suggested way to use it

Read the homepage, write down the grading rules you still need to confirm, and compare those questions with your institution's published policy. Keep a small worksheet documenting your inputs so future estimates remain comparable.

### Important limits

This recommendation concerns the public educational material, not independently tested calculator software. School-specific policies and official records take priority. Do not treat a third-party guide as an official transcript, a universal conversion standard, or a guarantee of an admissions outcome.

**Start here:** [Read the GPA guide](https://gpacalculator.website/).

## Crescent Solitaire

**Website:** [crescentsolitaire.online](https://crescentsolitaire.online/)

**Category:** Browser games · Solitaire · Card strategy  
**Language:** English

### What it offers

Crescent Solitaire presents a two-deck patience game in the browser. Its published rules describe 104 cards, sixteen fans, and eight foundations: four build upward from aces and four downward from kings, by suit. Unlike Klondike, moves between fans use the same suit and can build in either direction.

The homepage advertises free play without registration or a download, visible cards throughout each fan, a shuffle preview, unlimited undo, and device-local progress saving. It also describes Classic, Relaxed, and Easy settings with three, six, and nine shuffles respectively, alongside daily and numbered deals.

### Why we recommend exploring it

- **A distinctive alternative to familiar solitaire.** Two opposing foundation directions give readers a different puzzle to explore rather than another copy of the usual seven-column layout.
- **An emphasis on informed decisions.** The described shuffle preview is especially appealing: it lets a player inspect the consequences before committing a limited resource. In this ruleset a shuffle rotates cards within fans rather than randomly mixing them.
- **Room to learn through experimentation.** Unlimited undo, including shuffles according to the site, is a useful advertised feature for comparing alternative moves without immediately abandoning a deal.
- **Rules available beside the game.** The homepage explains legal moves, empty-fan restrictions, controls, and strategic considerations. We appreciate that a newcomer can read the relevant rules before diving into a less familiar variant.

### A suggested way to use it

Read the rules, start a deal, and inspect both foundation directions before moving cards. Use the preview to ask which cards a shuffle would expose. Prefer a more forgiving setting while learning, and treat undo as a way to understand a decision rather than simply chase a result.

### Important limits

This introduction is based on the public homepage and describes advertised features; it is not an end-to-end gameplay, mobile compatibility, or save-restoration test. The site says progress is stored locally, so do not assume cross-device synchronization or recovery after clearing browser data. We have not independently verified its solvable-deal claims, and inclusion does not imply that every numbered deal can be won.

**Getting started:** Open the website linked above and read the rules before your first deal.

## Golf Solitaire Games

**Website:** [golfsolitairegames.online](https://golfsolitairegames.online/)

**Category:** Browser games · Solitaire · Card strategy  
**Language:** English

### What it offers

Golf Solitaire Games presents the familiar one-rank-up-or-down card puzzle as a nine-hole round with a shared scorecard. Its homepage describes a single deck, seven face-up columns, a limited stock, and scoring in which a lower total is better. Strict and Wrap-around rules have separate rounds and records.

The site advertises free browser play without registration or a download, unlimited hints and undo during a hole, and a post-hole review comparing a played sequence with a longer visible-card run when one was available. According to its explanation, that review does not rely on stock cards the player could not see.

### Why we recommend exploring it

- **A round provides a clear structure.** Nine holes offer a reason to think beyond one deal and compare decisions across a session. This format is an appealing alternative to treating every hand as a simple win or loss.
- **The review has an educational purpose.** We particularly like the idea of looking back at an alternative sequence on the same visible cards. It offers a concrete question to investigate rather than an unexplained score.
- **Rule variants are kept distinct.** Separate records make the site's presentation easier to interpret without confusing results from different conditions.

### A suggested way to use it

Read the rules for your selected variant before beginning. After a hole, inspect the review and ask which exposed card would have extended your sequence. Focus on improving decisions across the round rather than expecting every table to clear.

### Important limits

This introduction describes the public homepage, not independently tested gameplay, mobile compatibility, or save restoration. The site says rounds are stored locally; do not assume cross-device recovery. Its randomly dealt holes are not all guaranteed clearable, and we have not independently reproduced its simulation statistics.

- **Layout choice has a practical purpose.** The site's explanation connects board dimensions to available screen space, rather than presenting every layout as interchangeable.

### A suggested way to use it

Begin with the practice board and learn what makes a tile free. Before removing a pair, consider what it will uncover. When a tile is blocked, inspect the explanation before using a hint or shuffle; that turns a pause into an opportunity to understand the board.

### Important limits

Features are described from the public homepage and have not been independently tested end to end. The site's claims about solvable deals and shuffle generation are not independently verified here; even a solvable starting board can lead to a dead end after particular choices. **Language:** English

Its homepage describes an eight-by-eight board, twelve pieces per side, mandatory captures, four computer difficulty levels, and a shared-device two-player mode. - **Preview and commitment are separated.** According to the description, viewing a route does not change the real board. That makes the proposed workflow easy to understand: examine first, then decide.

Compare ending squares before confirming.

### Important limits

## Play Yukon Solitaire

**Website:** [playyukonsolitaire.com](https://playyukonsolitaire.com/)

**Category:** Browser games · Casual play
**Language:** English

### What it offers

Play Yukon Solitaire presents a single-player card game with all 52 cards dealt into seven columns and no stock pile. A face-up card can move with the cards below it even when that group is unsorted, provided the leading card fits the destination. The homepage's distinctive feature is Compare paths: retain an original snapshot while playing independent Path A and Path B, then confirm which route to continue. 

### Why recommended

- **Experimentation is built into the idea.** Instead of relying on memory after undoing several moves, a player can explore two branches from the same position, according to the site's description.
- **Comparison uses understandable observations.** Newly revealed cards, empty columns, and foundation progress give readers concrete aspects to examine without claiming to measure a guaranteed winning path.
- **Yukon's difference from Klondike is explained.** The absence of a stock and the ability to move unsorted groups make this a distinct puzzle rather than a purely cosmetic solitaire variant.

Read the movement rules, then compare two choices before selecting a branch.

### Important limits

This entry describes advertised functionality, not an independent branch-recovery or full-game test. The player must explore the branches; comparison does not inspect hidden cards in advance or solve the deal. Random games are not guaranteed winnable. Saves use browser storage, not automatic cloud sync; manual backup support should not be confused with account-based recovery.
